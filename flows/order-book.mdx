title: Order book
description: Trade long and short tokens with other traders on an on-chain order book, at any hour, with market, limit, fill-or-kill and immediate-or-cancel orders, and a trading key that signs without a wallet prompt.
---

Every long and short token has an order book on [Manifest](https://github.com/Bonasa-Tech/manifest), a permissionless on-chain order book. That is 28 books, each quoting one token in cUSDC. You trade there with other traders. The protocol takes no side, charges no fee, and Manifest charges none either.

The books are open at any hour. They matter most while the underlying market is closed: the [bin books](/flows/trade#bin-books) keep trading then, but only up to a small capped size, and [committed orders](/flows/trade) can't settle because no price push arrives. On the order book, size is limited only by who is on the other side. While a market is closed the app shows the bin book's quotes and traders' orders as [one book](#one-book-while-closed).

<Note>
  Book prices never feed NAV, the oracle, γ or the CLP. A thin off-hours book is easy to push around, so nothing outside the book reads its prices.
</Note>

## One book while closed

While a market is closed, the app shows the bin book's quotes and traders' orders in one ladder and trades them as one book.

- A market order takes the cheapest offers first when you buy, and the highest bids first when you sell, whichever source holds them. At the same price, traders' orders fill before the pool.
- When both sources fill part of an order, it goes out as one transaction from your [trading account](/flows/trading-account): the pool's share through `swap_pool`, the rest as an immediate-or-cancel order on the book. If either part fails, neither happens.
- The [protection band](#protection-band) caps traders' offers, not the pool's quotes. After outside markets move, the pool's remaining side can sit outside the band and still be the honest price. A trader's offer outside the band fills only when it is cheaper than a pool quote the order takes anyway; on a sale, only when it bids more than one.
- A limit order priced through the pool's quote takes from the pool first, at the pool's price and never past your limit, and the rest rests on the book. A maker-only order priced through the pool's quote is refused, since it would fill at once.
- Your own resting orders show in the ladder, marked as yours, and your new orders skip them. Manifest has no self-trade prevention, so when a new order would match one of yours, the app cancels yours first, in the same transaction, and says so. Fill or kill can't be combined with that cancel: cancel your order first.

While a market is open, a market order is a [committed order](/flows/trade) at the next official price, unless traders' orders fill all of it now for less when you buy, or for more when you sell.

## Orders

| Order | What it does |
|---|---|
| Market | Fills now against resting orders and, while the market is closed, the pool's quotes. Traders' orders fill no worse than the [protection band](#protection-band) allows. Whatever can't fill inside that price is cancelled, not left on the book. |
| Market, fill or kill | The same, but the whole amount fills or nothing does. |
| Limit, until the reopen | Rests at your price and ends when the market reopens, so it can't be filled at a stale price once the first new print arrives. This is the default while a market is closed. |
| Limit, until cancelled | Rests until it fills or you cancel it. |
| Limit, fill now | Fills what it can at your price or better, then cancels the rest. |
| Maker only | A resting limit order that is cancelled instead of filling against the book when placed. The app refuses one priced through the pool's quote. |

Partial fills are normal on a book: a limit order can fill in several pieces as other traders arrive, and whatever is left keeps resting. Fills happen at the resting order's price, oldest first at each price.

A buy is sized in cUSDC to spend and a sell in tokens. A resting buy sets aside its limit price times its size until it fills or ends; a resting sell sets aside the tokens.

## Protection band

A market order takes traders' orders no worse than the leg's last posted price, widened by how far the price could plausibly have moved since that post:

```
band = 3 · σ · √(t / 30s) + jump        clamped to 1%–25%
```

- `σ` is the keeper's one-step GARCH volatility forecast, posted on-chain as the market's confidence.
- `t` is the time since the last price post. The band widens through a night and further over a weekend.
- Closed hours count at the rate prices actually move then, not the trading-hours rate. That is about a tenth of the trading-hours variance for stocks and indices, and a quarter for FX and metals, which keep trading somewhere until the weekend.
- `jump` is 10% for single stocks and VXX, which can gap on earnings and news. Indices, FX and metals get none.

Gold closed for a day and a half gets a band of about 2.5%. A single stock closed overnight gets about 13–15%, most of it the jump allowance. A limit order can sit anywhere. The app only warns when your price is outside the band.

The pool's own quotes aren't held to the band. After outside markets move, the keeper pulls the side that could be exploited, and the side it keeps can honestly sit outside the band. A market order's pool share is bounded by its slippage instead.

## Trading account and trading key

Book orders go through your [trading account](/flows/trading-account), the same account that trades the pool and committed orders. One wallet approval sets it up and puts a trading key in your browser. For 30 days the key places and cancels orders without a wallet prompt. It can't send funds anywhere except your own wallet.

A stolen key could still trade badly with an accomplice on the other side of the book. So the program holds every key-signed order to a wider backstop band, `3 · σ · √(t / 30s) + 10%` clamped to 10%–25% around the posted price. Orders your wallet signs are not checked.

If your trading account can't cover an order, the app moves the difference from your wallet in the same transaction and asks for one approval. After a fill, proceeds stay in the account, ready for the next order; **Withdraw** sends them to your wallet, with no prompt.

## Stops, take-profits and TWAP

These run in your browser tab, not on-chain. They only work while a tab with the app is open and online.

- A stop loss or take profit sends a market order when the best bid (for a sale) or best ask (for a buy) reaches your price: across the pool and traders' orders while the market is closed, and across traders' orders and a committed order's price while it's open. Your own orders don't count.
- A TWAP splits an order into equal parts over a period you choose.

Each fill is a market order signed by your trading key and filled like one you place by hand, with 1% slippage. They draw only on the trading account: a stop that would need a wallet approval fails instead of waiting for one.

## Spam and overload

Anyone can place orders on a Manifest book, so spam is the main risk to plan for.

- Resting orders cost the placer rent. Every resting order takes an 80-byte block of the book account. Whoever grows the account pays about 0.00056 SOL per block, and it isn't refunded.
- Expired orders are cleared by the next trade. An order past its last slot stays in the account until a trade walks into it, and that trade pays the compute to remove it. Measured against both builds of Manifest, removal costs 382–722 compute units per order. One transaction clears about 1,900–3,600 of them.
- A side can be blocked, at a price. Someone who parks more expired orders at the top of a side than one transaction can clear would block new trades on that side. Doing it costs them roughly 1–2 SOL of rent per side, which they never get back.
- Recovery is cheap. Funds in a blocked book are never stuck: withdrawals and cancels don't walk the book. The recovery is to list a fresh book for that token, about 0.007 SOL.
- Global orders fill like any other. A Manifest global order is backed by one deposit per token, shared across books, and its funds move only when it is matched, so a taker must pass that token's global accounts. Without them Manifest stops an immediate-or-cancel order at the first global order and fails a resting order that would cross one. The app passes them whenever the side an order takes holds a global order, and the trading program checks every address against the market.
