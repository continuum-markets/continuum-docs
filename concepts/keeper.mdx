title: Keeper
description: The off-chain service that pushes prices, manages the bin books, settles committed orders, and posts fees, buffers and volatility decay.
---

The **keeper** is an off-chain Rust service, run by the protocol, that drives Continuum's operational loops. Its source code is not published. This page describes how the protocol's keeper behaves and how it is configured.

1. The price updater pushes each market's price and confidence band on-chain, and withholds the push when the source goes stale.
2. The book manager (`clmm_pool.rs`) centres and reshapes the books, sizes the spread, refills and trims inventory, and posts the buffer, fees, committed spread and volatility decay.
3. The commit settler cranks committed-order settlement, which anyone may do.
4. The liquidity reallocator keeps each market's CLP vault at the cash it needs before its next top-up, holds the rest in the treasury, and backstops a market's collateral from the treasury when it falls below 100% or stays short of its target.
5. The yield allocator decides how much of each market's collateral to lend, and where. It lends only on mainnet, and only when switched on.

The keeper is **not in your transaction**. User mints, redeems and trades go straight to the on-chain programs. But mint and redeem rely on its pushes: both revert with `TwapNotAvailable` when the last push is more than 300 s old.

## Task inventory

### Price updater (every 5 s in the Docker image)

For each market, the keeper fetches a price and confidence band and pushes both with `mint_redeem::update_risk_state_v2`, together with the source's publish time. Pyth's Hermes service needs an API key (`PYTH_API_KEY`). Without one, the devnet keeper prices markets from a keyless public quote source and derives the band from a GJR-GARCH volatility model. Before it pushes a fresh price, it checks the price against Hyperliquid's oracle price for the same market and withholds the push if the two disagree. On devnet every market runs in `ProxyMode`. See [Oracle](/concepts/oracle).

The updater knows when a market is closed. If the source's publish time is more than 120 s old (`HERMES_STALE_SECS`), or the fetch fails, the keeper **withholds** the push. The stored price then ages past 300 s, mint and redeem freeze, and the book manager switches to free-float books.

### Book manager (30 s)

Per market, each tick:

- Feeds the vol state with the latest price, ignoring repeated closed-hours prices.
- Centres each book on NAV plus an **inventory shift** (the GLFT / Avellaneda-Stoikov centre shift). The shift is one dollar amount, added to one leg's centre and subtracted from the other's, so the pair price doesn't move: a lone long buyer pays a small premium that funds a lone short buyer's discount.
- **Repositions** a book with `set_pool_shape` when NAV drifts from its centre by more than a gate. The gate is 20 bps at most, cut down to each leg's funded quote edge.
- **Refills and trims** both ways. Drained asks restock (sweep bids → `vault_mint_pairs` → fund both sides). Bids hold at about \$5k per side: the excess goes to the CLP vault, and bids that sellers spent are refilled from it (`clp_fund_pool_usdc`). Asks trim to at most 1.5× that \$5k target. Nothing refills while a book floats off-hours.
- Posts one **free-float book** per side off-hours, anchored at the last price. Each side puts all its depth in one bin, the outermost within 1% of that price: the r55 study found that bins further out took no ordinary flow and only carried gap risk. The depth is sized to demand, max(\$500, the 99.9th percentile of one side's measured flow per closed period), under a ceiling of \$10,000 per side (\$1,250 for the single-name stocks). Until the keeper measures its own fills, flow comes from `CLMM_FREEFLOAT_FLOW_USD_<SYM>` and defaults to zero, so every side posts \$500. A guard can then pull the bin on the side that other venues show is stale. It never moves the centre or adds depth.
- Posts each market's **buffer target** (`set_buffer_target`): the daily Monte Carlo target from the market's live state, or a fixed fallback if that is over 48 hours old. The same nightly job publishes each market's CLP stock share for imbalance control (see [Solvency](/concepts/solvency#3-posted-overcollateralization-buffer)), only when it differs from the value on chain. Posts the **mint/redeem fee** (`update_market`, `clamp(fee_mult × half-spread, 10, 100)` bps, the same value for both) and the **committed spread** (`set_commit_spread`, `clamp(half-spread, 10, 500)` bps) once they move by at least 5 and 2 bps respectively. The keeper posts a committed spread on all 13 markets, and since 11 Oct 2026 every one accepts committed orders.
- Posts the **short-leg volatility decay** (`set_s_gamma_index`). It is always on and has no switch. Charges accrue until they reach a 2e-4 posting gate, and the unposted balance is saved to R2 or `DATA_DIR`, so a restart doesn't discard it. A market that hasn't had a γ write for a day is posted anyway once at least 1e-6 is pending, so slow markets such as FX are charged at least daily. See [NAV → Volatility decay](/concepts/nav#volatility-decay).
- Runs the **cross-market collateral rebalance**. It plans at the price the program checks, which is the stored price with γ applied to the short leg, and leaves each source one confidence band of margin above its target. A market within one confidence band of its target is left alone, since the next price can close that gap. Past the band it is filled to its target plus the band. Markets below 100% collateralization are filled first, then markets below their target, and each source's excess is split across the markets it can reach in proportion to their need. The program clamps each move to the source's excess above its own target.

### Commit settler (20 s idle, 5 s while orders pend)

The settler finds pending `Order` accounts with one filtered `getProgramAccounts` call, and also wakes early on websocket notifications. It settles up to 10 orders per tick, largest first, and simulates each settle before sending it:

- A refund (a `min_out` miss), or a fill the CLP vault already covers, is sent unchanged.
- A fill the CLP can't cover is funded first. The settler borrows the cash it lacks from the treasury (`allocate_to_market`) and mints or redeems pairs if needed: a sell pays out cUSDC, and a buy needs synth, which the CLP mints in pairs at twice the value it lacks. These steps go in the same transaction as the settle where they fit, so a fill that fails takes its funding back with it. Otherwise they run in sequence before the settle.
- An order that the collateral floor or the oracle checks hold back waits for a later push.

About 5 s after an order's 180 s TTL, the settler refunds it with `cancel_swap`, which returns the escrow to the owner. Anyone may settle or refund; the keeper just does it promptly.

### Liquidity reallocator (30 s)

The CLP's cUSDC sits in the treasury (the global vault) and in each market's CLP vault. The reallocator is the only task that moves it between them. A CLP vault holds only working cash: what the market's instant consumers can spend before the next tick, each figure taken from that consumer's own setting:

- Both books' bid refills: 2 × `CLMM_BID_TARGET_USD` for each book-manager tick in one reallocator tick (\$10,000).
- CLP stock operations: a \$10,000 reserve plus \$20,000 that funds imbalance control's mint fallback. It used to fund the inventory restocker, which kept about \$10,000 a side for the retired instant swap and is off since 12 Oct 2026.

That comes to \$40,000 per market. Each vault is kept between that need and twice it. Below the need, it is topped up to 1.5×. Above twice the need, everything over 1.5× returns to the treasury. Every move is at least half a tick's spend, and even at the full spend rate a market needs at most one top-up per tick. An inactive market keeps nothing.

The first pass, on 11 Oct 2026, returned \$12.99M from 11 markets, \$1.18M each, and left them \$60,000 apiece. QQQ, SPY and XAU also held about \$1.04M each for the instant swap's rolling-window cap. Once `clp_swap` was retired later that day, the allowance went, and they returned \$3.6M to the treasury between them. The treasury held \$22.13M after those returns.

Committed orders need no standing cash, because the settler draws from the treasury when they settle. A shared lock stops the reallocator from returning that cash before the orders settle. Cash waiting in the treasury is available to any market, and the yield allocator counts it as first-loss capital.

#### Treasury backstop

The reallocator also tops up market collateral from the treasury, with `backstop_market`, which pays into the market's collateral rather than its CLP vault. It reads each market's collateral ratio at the stored price:

- Below 100%, it fills at once, at least up to the market's target. Committed orders refuse to fill below the 100% floor, so this is what lets them resume.
- Short of its target by more than one confidence band for 5 minutes, it fills to the target plus the band. That is the cross-market rebalancer's destination rule, and the rebalancer moves other markets' excess first.

The target the treasury fills to is the market's posted target, capped at 130%. MSTR, closed on 12 Oct 2026, gets none. Fills are not returned automatically: a later surplus stays as a cushion, which the rebalancer can move between markets.

### Treasury withdrawal rate (900 s)

`admin_withdraw` can take only what the treasury policy's allowance holds, and the allowance refills at a set rate. This task keeps that rate in proportion to the balance, so a full exit takes 24 hours whatever the treasury holds: an hour's worth at once, the rest at the rate. On 12 Oct 2026 the treasury held \$23.11M, and the rate was still the 64.06 cUSDC a second set when it held \$5.53M. The task proposed 256.78 cUSDC a second with a 924,403 cUSDC burst, which applies at 01:25:50 UTC on 13 Oct. The treasury has since grown to \$40.0M (22:26 UTC, 12 Oct), so at the current rate a full exit takes about 7.2 days.

The program decides how a change lands. A cut applies at once. A raise waits out the policy's 24 h delay, during which the guardian can cancel it, and the task applies it once due, so a deposit can't speed up withdrawals any sooner. The task changes nothing while the allowance is refilling, after a withdrawal in the last hour or a raise just applied, so the rate in force when an exit starts holds until it ends. It moves the limits only when they are more than 10% off, and it leaves alone any pending change that alters the destination, guardian or delay.

### Imbalance control (900 s)

After the keeper's γ charge, a price move changes a market's claims by exactly (L − S) times the return, where L and S are the values of all its long and all its short tokens. Nothing moves the anchor, so the imbalance β = (L − S)/(L + S) drifts with the price, and the posted buffer has to cover a year of that drift. This task keeps β near zero with the protocol's own tokens. When β leaves its band (0.05), it redeems the CLP's surplus of the heavier leg at NAV through `vault_redeem_single`. Collateral and claims fall by the same amount and no fee is charged, so no holder's tokens change value. The cash lands in the CLP vault, and the reallocator returns it to the treasury.

It acts only on a price under 240 s old, so not while a market is closed, and redeems at most \$50,000 per market per tick, nothing under \$100. It also owns the CLP's own stock. Each leg keeps a share of the market's claims, which the daily Monte Carlo job re-derives every night (0.5% until it has). It never redeems a leg below that share, and when both legs sit above it, it trims the matched excess with fee-free redemptions of equal value. The share is the cheapest trade-off between capital held and mint fees saved. It is computed from that night's re-centring events at the current volatility, 30 days of measured committed-order volume and the live mint fees. Yesterday's value stays while it is within 10% of the night's best. When the CLP holds too little of the heavier leg, it first mints pairs from the CLP vault. A pair mint splits the collateral 50/50 by value, so each leg gains the shortfall. Then it redeems the heavier leg's surplus: in effect a mint of the lighter leg. That costs about twice the mint fee on the value added, and 90% of the fee stays in the market's collateral. The vault keeps \$10,000 for the books' sell payouts.

It has run live since 12 Oct 2026, after a few hours in dry-run. The daily collateral targets plan on it: each market's buffer is computed with the book re-centred whenever its imbalance leaves the band, for as long as the live imbalance stays within twice the band (see [Solvency](/concepts/solvency#3-posted-overcollateralization-buffer)). A market whose imbalance gets past that is planned as if nothing re-centred it.

### Yield allocator (300 s)

The allocator is the only part of the keeper that lends collateral. Each tick it reads the USDC reserves of four mainnet venues straight from chain: Kamino's main market, MarginFi, Save's main pool and Jupiter Lend Earn. From each reserve it rebuilds the venue's interest-rate curve, so it knows how far the supply rate falls as the protocol's own deposit pushes utilization down. It plans on a blended rate, 25% today's rate and 75% the venue's 30-day mean, so a one-day spike doesn't pull collateral.

Its limits are derived from stated assumptions:

| Limit | Rule | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid share | The vault keeps one recall cycle of net redemptions: the larger of a measured share s and the stressed run over the recall horizon, q = max(s, 1 − (1 − r)^(T / 24 h)) | s = 3% (`YIELD_LIQUID_SHARE`). Continuum has no flow yet, so r59 measured the closest analog, Kamino Main USDC's 9,009 hourly net flows: paying 99% of 5-minute windows instantly needs 1.7–6% of the deposit base, 3.2% at the measured 1 h to 24 h scaling. r = 50% a day and T = 0.1 h give only 0.3%, so s binds. There is no fixed-dollar floor: it was \$10,000, twice the CLP's underived \$5,000. With recall inside the redeem transaction, s can go to 0 |
| Venue cap | Each venue's total position, summed over all markets, is at most its share of first-loss capital, set by its record: half at Kamino and Jupiter Lend, which have no lender losses on record, and a quarter at Save and MarginFi. Any two venues can fail outright and every market stays fully backed | First-loss capital = the treasury + 50% of all markets' collateral above 100% |
| Venue exit cap | The protocol can always get out: at most half the venue's withdrawable liquidity, and never past its deposit cap | 50% |
| Hurdle | Stop adding once the marginal rate falls below it | 1% APR |
| Switching | Move only when the gain over 30 days, after a 25 bps charge for rate noise on anything withdrawn, beats twice the transaction cost | 2× |

First-loss capital is the capital that can absorb a venue failure. It is the treasury, which the allocator reads on chain every tick, plus 50% of all markets' collateral above 100% (`YIELD_VENUE_LOSS_BUDGET`). The treasury counts because `backstop_market` can move it into any market's collateral. With the shares at their defaults, any two venues can lose everything at once and `backstop_market` restores the markets. Save's record is a \$1.26M bad debt in three isolated pools in November 2022, which a DAO vote repaid; MarginFi's is a critical flash-loan bug, reported through its bug bounty and patched before anyone used it. On 12 Oct 2026 the shares bound nothing. Kamino, Save and MarginFi were held by their own exit liquidity, and only Jupiter Lend's cap came from its share.

Per market, only the liquidity budget binds: the 3% liquid share. The on-chain rails (`yield_enabled` and `yield_ceiling_bps`) apply on top. A market's ledger has three venue entries, so one market holds at most three venues at a time. The allocator assigns those entries across markets so that between them the markets reach all four venues. A market at or below 100% lends nothing.

Inside those limits it fills the venues in order of marginal rate, so every funded venue ends at about the same marginal rate. When the venues can't take every market's full budget, each market lends the same share of its budget, so every market keeps the same liquid share. A market in Stress or Recovery recalls everything.

Until 11 Oct 2026 the venue cap was half of each market's own collateral above 100%. At the 102% target that left about 3% of collateral lendable. On 11 Oct 2026 the new rule was measured against live mainnet venues for the 14 devnet markets, which hold \$19.2M of collateral. With the \$5.53M funded treasury, the plan lent about two-thirds of that collateral, held back by Jupiter Lend's venue cap of \$5.7M. Later that day the liquidity reallocator returned \$12.99M of idle CLP cash, taking the treasury to \$18.52M and first-loss capital to \$18.73M. The plan now lends 69.9%, the same share in every market, and earns 3.07% on all of it, about \$589,000 a year. Each market's liquid share is what binds now, not venue capacity. Kamino and MarginFi stop at their exit caps, and Jupiter Lend and Save share the rest where their marginal rates meet. On 12 Oct 2026 the protocol's own holdings were cut to working size, leaving about \$1.4M of collateral. At that size no venue cap binds except MarginFi's small exit cap, and each market's liquid share decides what is lent. That size was chosen partly because the venues couldn't absorb much more: at a \$1M to \$5M treasury, the loss shares would let only 10% to 38% of the old \$18.3M be lent.

It sends transactions only on mainnet USDC with `COLLATERAL_YIELD_ENABLED=true`, which is off by default. On every other cluster it plans and reports to the dashboard but sends nothing. The on-chain rails sit on top: `yield_enabled` and `yield_ceiling_bps` on each market. Since 12 Oct 2026 every market has yield enabled with its ceiling at 10,000 bps: all idle collateral may be lent, and the keeper recalls it before paying out. The program accepted at most 9,000 bps until then. Study r57 had recommended 9,000, because a 10% cash share pays a 10% run even on a day the lending venues freeze (as Solend's did in June 2022, during FTX and in July 2023); the operator chose 100%.

Devnet has no real lending, so a yield emulator (`DEVNET_YIELD_EMULATION`, default on, devnet only) stands in for it. Every hour it mints the cUSDC that the allocator's mainnet plan would have earned on each market's collateral, and pushes it through the real `harvest_collateral_yield`, which adds it to the market's `total_collateral`. When no fresh mainnet snapshot is available, it credits nothing for that hour. The emulator sizes its plan against the same treasury, so emulated yield rose with the new venue cap.

### Housekeeping

SOL balance checks every 300 s (with alerts, and airdrop top-ups on devnet), an inventory monitor, and an operator dashboard.

## The spread engine (model-free)

The half-spread that shapes every book is **not a forecasting model**. No trained model is in the live path: a linear-quantile forecaster measured net-negative in research, because inside a `max()` with the floors it could only widen, taxing flow without adding protection. The live spread is a max-of-floors stack:

```
half_spread = max(
    realized-q90 ratchet,    # trailing empirical q90 of window |moves|;
                             # warms within ~8 windows
    floor_mult × σ_60d,      # slow auto-floor from 60-day unconditional vol;
                             # self-corrects mis-set listing floors
    oracle_conf + fixed,     # never quote inside oracle uncertainty
    min_bps,                 # per-market static boot floor:
)                            # QQQ 18, SPY 15, XAU 18, NVDA 30
```

Two post-processing steps follow. Above `freeze_trigger`, the book **freezes**: it stops repositioning and floats rather than chasing a disorderly tape. And a **protective drift skew** widens the trend-adverse side by ×(1 + z), where z is a slow, roughly 1-day return z-score, while the other side **never tightens**. Backtested, the skew fixes trend bleed (XAU went from −233 to +257 bps/yr). The antisymmetric variant, which also tightened the other side, was falsified.

The vol state lives in memory. After a restart, the `min_bps` and oracle-confidence floors govern until the ratchet warms, within about 8 windows; the slow floor takes longer. Closed-hours repeated prices are ignored.

### Listing without model training

Per-market config (`min_bps` / `floor_mult` / `fee_mult` / window) lives in the keeper's config JSON; `fee_mult` is 1.0 on every live market. Listing a new asset needs **no training**: the floors plus the ratchet carry both safety and competitiveness. The recipe is steep-wing geometry sized to the asset's vol class, `min_bps` at least about 2× its 5-minute σ, and `fee_mult` around 1.0 to 1.5 for high-vol single names.

## Configuration

Most keeper behavior sits behind env switches that default on. γ decay has none.

| Switch | Default | Gates |
|---|---|---|
| `CLMM_POOLS_ENABLED` | on | Book management as a whole |
| `CLMM_VOL_SPREAD_ENABLED` | on | Vol-scaled spread (off → static floors) |
| `CLMM_INVENTORY_SKEW_ENABLED` | on | Inventory centre shift |
| `CLMM_BUFFER_POST_ENABLED` | on | Buffer-target posting |
| `CLMM_FEE_SCALE_ENABLED` | on | Vol-informed fee posting |
| `CLMM_COMMIT_SPREAD_ENABLED` | on | Committed-spread posting |
| `CLMM_REBALANCE_ENABLED` | on | Cross-market collateral rebalance |
| `CLMM_REFILL_ENABLED` | on | Refill (asks and bids) and trim |
| `FF_GUARD_ENABLED` | on | Off-hours free-float guard |
| `COMMIT_SETTLE_ENABLED` | on | Settlement crank |
| `LIQUIDITY_REALLOCATOR_MODE` | `live` | Treasury ↔ CLP-vault moves (`off`, `dryrun` or `live`) |
| `YIELD_ALLOCATOR_ENABLED` | on | Yield planning, on every cluster |
| `COLLATERAL_YIELD_ENABLED` | off | Lending collateral (mainnet USDC only) |
| `DEVNET_YIELD_EMULATION` | on (devnet only) | Devnet yield emulator |

The keyless fallback price source has its own switch, which is off by default. The devnet keeper runs with it on.

Main tunables:

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `PYTH_API_KEY` | unset | Pyth Hermes API key. Without it, Pyth requests fail and the keyless fallback, if enabled, prices the market |
| `GAMMA_DECAY_COEF` | `1.0` | Scales the γ charge; must be in (0, 1]. Setting `CLMM_GAMMA_DECAY_ENABLED` to any value aborts boot |
| `UPDATE_INTERVAL_MS` | `15000` (`5000` in the Docker image) | Price updater cadence |
| `HERMES_STALE_SECS` | `120` | Source-price age at which a push is withheld, for every source |
| `CLMM_REPOSITION_BPS[_SYM]` | `20` | Largest drift gate before a reposition |
| `CLMM_BID_TARGET_USD` | `5000` | Bid-side cUSDC per side: swept above it, refilled below it |
| `CLMM_TRIM_RATIO` | `1.5` | Max asks-to-bid-target value ratio |
| `LIQUIDITY_REALLOCATOR_INTERVAL_SECS` | `30` | Reallocator cadence (min 30). A longer tick widens each vault's band |
| `COMMIT_SETTLE_INTERVAL_SECS` | `20` | Settler cadence when idle |
| `PORT`, `DASHBOARD_PORT` | `8484` | Dashboard port (`PORT` wins, then `DASHBOARD_PORT`) |

## Can I run my own keeper?

Not the protocol's: its source isn't published, and its privileged instructions check the market's authorities. What's open and what isn't:

- Anyone can crank `settle_swap`. Settling committed orders needs no authority.
- Paired arbitrage is open. Ordinary `mint_paired` / `redeem_paired` plus book trades need no privilege, and neither does `donate_to_vault`.
- Pricing and book shaping are privileged. `update_risk_state_v2`, `set_buffer_target` and `set_s_gamma_index` require `market.keeper_authority`. `set_pool_shape` requires the pool's `authority`, fee posting through `update_market` requires `market.authority`, and `set_commit_spread` requires the CLP's `authority`.

## Dashboard

The keeper serves an operator dashboard on one HTTP port (`PORT`, then `DASHBOARD_PORT`, default 8484). It shows price freshness per market, risk state, book positions and drift, pending committed orders, vault balances and yield status. It is for the operator; integrators read state from on-chain accounts.
