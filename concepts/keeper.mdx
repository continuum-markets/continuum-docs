title: Keeper
description: The off-chain service that pushes prices, manages the bin books, settles committed orders, and posts fees, buffers and volatility decay.
---

The **keeper** is an off-chain Rust service, run by the protocol, that drives Continuum's operational loops. Its source code is not published. This page describes how the protocol's keeper behaves and how it is configured.

1. The price updater pushes each market's price and confidence band on-chain, and withholds the push when the source goes stale.
2. The book manager (`clmm_pool.rs`) centres and reshapes the books, sizes the spread, refills and trims inventory, and posts the buffer, fees, committed spread and volatility decay.
3. The commit settler cranks committed-order settlement, which anyone may do.
4. The liquidity reallocator keeps each market's CLP vault at the cash it needs before its next top-up and holds the rest in the treasury.
5. The yield allocator decides how much of each market's collateral to lend, and where. It lends only on mainnet, and only when switched on.

The keeper is **not in your transaction**. User mints, redeems and trades go straight to the on-chain programs. But mint and redeem rely on its pushes: both revert with `TwapNotAvailable` when the last push is more than 300 s old.

## Task inventory

### Price updater (every 5 s in the Docker image)

For each market, the keeper fetches a price and confidence band and pushes both with `mint_redeem::update_risk_state_v2`, together with the source's publish time. Pyth's Hermes service needs an API key (`PYTH_API_KEY`). Without one, the devnet keeper prices markets from a keyless public quote source and derives the band from a GJR-GARCH volatility model. Before it pushes a fresh price, it checks the price against Hyperliquid's oracle price for the same market and withholds the push if the two disagree. On devnet every market runs in `ProxyMode`. See [Oracle](/concepts/oracle).

The updater knows when a market is closed. If the source's publish time is more than 120 s old (`HERMES_STALE_SECS`), or the fetch fails, the keeper **withholds** the push. The stored price then ages past 300 s, mint and redeem freeze, and the book manager switches to free-float books.

### Book manager (30 s)

Per market, each tick:

- Feeds the vol state with the latest price, ignoring repeated closed-hours prices.
- Centres each book on NAV plus an **inventory shift** (the GLFT / Avellaneda-Stoikov centre shift). The shift is one dollar amount, added to one leg's centre and subtracted from the other's, so the pair price doesn't move: a lone long buyer pays a small premium that funds a lone short buyer's discount.
- **Repositions** a book with `set_pool_shape` when NAV drifts from its centre by more than a gate. The gate is 20 bps at most, cut down to each leg's funded quote edge.
- **Refills and trims** both ways. Drained asks restock (sweep bids → `vault_mint_pairs` → fund both sides). Bids hold at about \$5k per side: the excess goes to the CLP vault, and bids that sellers spent are refilled from it (`clp_fund_pool_usdc`). Asks trim to at most 1.5× that \$5k target. Nothing refills while a book floats off-hours.
- Posts one **free-float book** per side off-hours, anchored at the last price, flat across the ladder, and capped at \$10,000 per side (\$1,250 for the single-name stocks). A guard can then pull bins on the side that other venues show is stale. It never moves the centre or adds depth.
- Posts the **dynamic buffer** target (`set_buffer_target`), the **mint/redeem fee** (`update_market`, `clamp(fee_mult × half-spread, 10, 100)` bps, the same value for both) and the **committed spread** (`set_commit_spread`, `clamp(half-spread, 10, 500)` bps) once they move by at least 50, 5 and 2 bps respectively. The keeper posts a committed spread on all 14 markets, but as of 10 Oct 2026 only QQQ, SPY and XAU accept committed orders.
- Posts the **short-leg volatility decay** (`set_s_gamma_index`). It is always on and has no switch. Charges accrue until they reach a 2e-4 posting gate, and the unposted balance is saved to R2 or `DATA_DIR`, so a restart doesn't discard it. A market that hasn't had a γ write for a day is posted anyway once at least 1e-6 is pending, so slow markets such as FX are charged at least daily. See [NAV → Volatility decay](/concepts/nav#volatility-decay).
- Runs the **cross-market collateral rebalance**. It plans at the price the program checks, which is the stored price with γ applied to the short leg, and leaves each source one confidence band of margin above its target. A market within one confidence band of its target is left alone, since the next price can close that gap. Past the band it is filled to its target plus the band. Markets below 100% collateralization are filled first, then markets below their target, and each source's excess is split across the markets it can reach in proportion to their need. The program clamps each move to the source's excess above its own target.

### Commit settler (20 s idle, 5 s while orders pend)

The settler finds pending `Order` accounts with one filtered `getProgramAccounts` call, and also wakes early on websocket notifications. It provisions inventory, then settles up to 10 orders per tick, largest first. Sells pay out cUSDC. Buys need synth, which the CLP mints in pairs at twice the value it lacks. The CLP vault keeps only working cash, so the settler draws whatever the orders need beyond it from the treasury (`allocate_to_market`). A sell gap the treasury can't cover falls back to redeeming CLP-held pairs. Anyone may settle; the keeper just does it promptly.

### Liquidity reallocator (30 s)

The CLP's cUSDC sits in the treasury (the global vault) and in each market's CLP vault. The reallocator is the only task that moves it between them. A CLP vault holds what the market's instant consumers can spend before the next tick, each figure taken from that consumer's own setting:

- Both books' bid refills: 2 × `CLMM_BID_TARGET_USD` for each book-manager tick in one reallocator tick (\$10,000).
- The inventory restock: its \$10,000 payout reserve plus one \$20,000 restock cycle.
- Instant swaps (`clp_swap`), on markets whose swap guards are on: the rolling-window notional cap, since one swap can spend all of it (\$1M an hour on QQQ, SPY and XAU).

That comes to \$40,000 on 11 markets and \$1.04M on QQQ, SPY and XAU. Each vault is kept between that need and twice it. Below the need, it is topped up to 1.5×. Above twice the need, everything over 1.5× returns to the treasury. Every move is at least half a tick's spend, and even at the full spend rate a market needs at most one top-up per tick. An inactive market keeps nothing.

The first pass, on 11 Oct 2026, returned \$12.99M from 11 markets, \$1.18M each, and left them \$60,000 apiece. QQQ, SPY and XAU kept about \$1.25M each for their instant-swap cap.

Committed orders need no standing cash, because the settler draws from the treasury when they settle. A shared lock stops the reallocator from returning that cash before the orders settle. Cash waiting in the treasury is available to any market, and the yield allocator counts it as first-loss capital.

### Yield allocator (300 s)

The allocator is the only part of the keeper that lends collateral. Each tick it reads the USDC reserves of four mainnet venues straight from chain: Kamino's main market, MarginFi, Save's main pool and Jupiter Lend Earn. From each reserve it rebuilds the venue's interest-rate curve, so it knows how far the supply rate falls as the protocol's own deposit pushes utilization down. It plans on a blended rate, 25% today's rate and 75% the venue's 30-day mean, so a one-day spike doesn't pull collateral.

Its limits are derived from stated assumptions:

| Limit | Rule | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid share | The vault keeps enough to meet a stressed redemption run until a recall lands: q = 1 − (1 − r)^(T / 24 h) | r = 50% a day and T = 12 h give q ≈ 29.3%, plus a $10,000 floor |
| Venue cap | Each venue's total position, summed over all markets, is at most first-loss capital ÷ the number of venue failures to cover. That many venues can fail outright and every market stays fully backed | First-loss capital = the treasury + 50% of all markets' collateral above 100%; 1 failure covered |
| Venue exit cap | The protocol can always get out: at most half the venue's withdrawable liquidity, and never past its deposit cap | 50% |
| Hurdle | Stop adding once the marginal rate falls below it | 1% APR |
| Switching | Move only when the gain over 30 days, after a 25 bps charge for rate noise on anything withdrawn, beats twice the transaction cost | 2× |

First-loss capital is the capital that can absorb a venue failure. It is the treasury, which the allocator reads on chain every tick, plus 50% of all markets' collateral above 100% (`YIELD_VENUE_LOSS_BUDGET`). The treasury counts because `backstop_market` can move it into any market's collateral. With `YIELD_VENUE_FAILURES_COVERED` at its default of 1, any single venue can lose everything and `backstop_market` restores the markets.

Per market, only the liquidity budget binds: the liquid share of about 29.3% plus the \$10,000 floor. The on-chain rails (`yield_enabled` and `yield_ceiling_bps`) apply on top. A market's ledger has three venue entries, so one market holds at most three venues at a time. The allocator assigns those entries across markets so that between them the markets reach all four venues. A market at or below 100% lends nothing.

Inside those limits it fills the venues in order of marginal rate, so every funded venue ends at about the same marginal rate. When the venues can't take every market's full budget, each market lends the same share of its budget, so every market keeps the same liquid share. A market in Stress or Recovery recalls everything.

Until 11 Oct 2026 the venue cap was half of each market's own collateral above 100%. At the 102% target that left about 3% of collateral lendable. On 11 Oct 2026 the new rule was measured against live mainnet venues for the 14 devnet markets, which hold \$19.2M of collateral. With the \$5.53M funded treasury, the plan lent about two-thirds of that collateral, held back by Jupiter Lend's venue cap of \$5.7M. Later that day the liquidity reallocator returned \$12.99M of idle CLP cash, taking the treasury to \$18.52M and first-loss capital to \$18.73M. The plan now lends 69.9%, the same share in every market, and earns 3.07% on all of it, about \$589,000 a year. Each market's liquid share is what binds now, not venue capacity. Kamino and MarginFi stop at their exit caps, and Jupiter Lend and Save share the rest where their marginal rates meet.

It sends transactions only on mainnet USDC with `COLLATERAL_YIELD_ENABLED=true`, which is off by default. On every other cluster it plans and reports to the dashboard but sends nothing. The on-chain rails sit on top: `yield_enabled` and `yield_ceiling_bps` on each market. As of 11 Oct 2026 every market has yield enabled, with its ceiling set to the derived liquid share, about 7,000 bps.

Devnet has no real lending, so a yield emulator (`DEVNET_YIELD_EMULATION`, default on, devnet only) stands in for it. Every hour it mints the cUSDC that the allocator's mainnet plan would have earned on each market's collateral, and pushes it through the real `harvest_collateral_yield`, which adds it to the market's `total_collateral`. When no fresh mainnet snapshot is available, it credits nothing for that hour. The emulator sizes its plan against the same treasury, so emulated yield rose with the new venue cap.

### Housekeeping

SOL balance checks every 300 s (with alerts, and airdrop top-ups on devnet), an inventory monitor, and an operator dashboard.

## The spread engine (model-free)

The half-spread that shapes every book is **not a forecasting model**. No trained model is in the live path: a linear-quantile forecaster measured net-negative in research, because inside a `max()` with the floors it could only widen, taxing flow without adding protection. The live spread is a max-of-floors stack:

```
half_spread = max(
    realized-q90 ratchet,    # trailing empirical q90 of window |moves|;
                             # warms within ~8 windows
    floor_mult × σ_60d,      # slow auto-floor from 60-day unconditional vol;
                             # self-corrects mis-set listing floors
    oracle_conf + fixed,     # never quote inside oracle uncertainty
    min_bps,                 # per-market static boot floor:
)                            # QQQ 18, SPY 15, XAU 18, NVDA 30
```

Two post-processing steps follow. Above `freeze_trigger`, the book **freezes**: it stops repositioning and floats rather than chasing a disorderly tape. And a **protective drift skew** widens the trend-adverse side by ×(1 + z), where z is a slow, roughly 1-day return z-score, while the other side **never tightens**. Backtested, the skew fixes trend bleed (XAU went from −233 to +257 bps/yr). The antisymmetric variant, which also tightened the other side, was falsified.

The vol state lives in memory. After a restart, the `min_bps` and oracle-confidence floors govern until the ratchet warms, within about 8 windows; the slow floor takes longer. Closed-hours repeated prices are ignored.

### Listing without model training

Per-market config (`min_bps` / `floor_mult` / `fee_mult` / window) lives in the keeper's config JSON; `fee_mult` is 1.0 on every live market. Listing a new asset needs **no training**: the floors plus the ratchet carry both safety and competitiveness. The recipe is steep-wing geometry sized to the asset's vol class, `min_bps` at least about 2× its 5-minute σ, and `fee_mult` around 1.0 to 1.5 for high-vol single names.

## Configuration

Most keeper behavior sits behind env switches that default on. γ decay has none.

| Switch | Default | Gates |
|---|---|---|
| `CLMM_POOLS_ENABLED` | on | Book management as a whole |
| `CLMM_VOL_SPREAD_ENABLED` | on | Vol-scaled spread (off → static floors) |
| `CLMM_INVENTORY_SKEW_ENABLED` | on | Inventory centre shift |
| `CLMM_BUFFER_POST_ENABLED` | on | Dynamic buffer posting |
| `CLMM_FEE_SCALE_ENABLED` | on | Vol-informed fee posting |
| `CLMM_COMMIT_SPREAD_ENABLED` | on | Committed-spread posting |
| `CLMM_REBALANCE_ENABLED` | on | Cross-market collateral rebalance |
| `CLMM_REFILL_ENABLED` | on | Refill (asks and bids) and trim |
| `FF_GUARD_ENABLED` | on | Off-hours free-float guard |
| `COMMIT_SETTLE_ENABLED` | on | Settlement crank |
| `LIQUIDITY_REALLOCATOR_MODE` | `live` | Treasury ↔ CLP-vault moves (`off`, `dryrun` or `live`) |
| `YIELD_ALLOCATOR_ENABLED` | on | Yield planning, on every cluster |
| `COLLATERAL_YIELD_ENABLED` | off | Lending collateral (mainnet USDC only) |
| `DEVNET_YIELD_EMULATION` | on (devnet only) | Devnet yield emulator |

The keyless fallback price source has its own switch, which is off by default. The devnet keeper runs with it on.

Main tunables:

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `PYTH_API_KEY` | unset | Pyth Hermes API key. Without it, Pyth requests fail and the keyless fallback, if enabled, prices the market |
| `GAMMA_DECAY_COEF` | `1.0` | Scales the γ charge; must be in (0, 1]. Setting `CLMM_GAMMA_DECAY_ENABLED` to any value aborts boot |
| `UPDATE_INTERVAL_MS` | `15000` (`5000` in the Docker image) | Price updater cadence |
| `HERMES_STALE_SECS` | `120` | Source-price age at which a push is withheld, for every source |
| `CLMM_REPOSITION_BPS[_SYM]` | `20` | Largest drift gate before a reposition |
| `CLMM_BID_TARGET_USD` | `5000` | Bid-side cUSDC per side: swept above it, refilled below it |
| `CLMM_TRIM_RATIO` | `1.5` | Max asks-to-bid-target value ratio |
| `LIQUIDITY_REALLOCATOR_INTERVAL_SECS` | `30` | Reallocator cadence (min 30). A longer tick widens each vault's band |
| `COMMIT_SETTLE_INTERVAL_SECS` | `20` | Settler cadence when idle |
| `PORT`, `DASHBOARD_PORT` | `8484` | Dashboard port (`PORT` wins, then `DASHBOARD_PORT`) |

Full list: [keeper running guide](/keeper/running).

## Can I run my own keeper?

Not the protocol's: its source isn't published, and its privileged instructions check the market's authorities. What's open and what isn't:

- Anyone can crank `settle_swap`. Settling committed orders needs no authority.
- Paired arbitrage is open. Ordinary `mint_paired` / `redeem_paired` plus book trades need no privilege, and neither does `donate_to_vault`.
- Pricing and book shaping are privileged. `update_risk_state_v2`, `set_buffer_target` and `set_s_gamma_index` require `market.keeper_authority`. `set_pool_shape` requires the pool's `authority`, fee posting through `update_market` requires `market.authority`, and `set_commit_spread` requires the CLP's `authority`.

## Dashboard

The keeper serves an operator dashboard on one HTTP port (`PORT`, then `DASHBOARD_PORT`, default 8484). It shows price freshness per market, risk state, book positions and drift, pending committed orders, vault balances and yield status. It is for the operator; integrators read state from on-chain accounts.
