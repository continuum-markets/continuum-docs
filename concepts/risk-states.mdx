---
title: Risk states
description: The per-market risk regime, what each state decides, how the price band and the fee work, why devnet runs in ProxyMode, and the staleness gate that freezes mint and redeem off-hours.
---

Every market carries a `RiskState` on its [`Market`](/programs/mint-redeem#market-account) account. The keeper writes it with each price push (`update_risk_state_v2`). The state says which price mint and redeem use and whether they are open at all. It no longer changes the fee or the price band. A separate staleness gate freezes mint and redeem when pushes stop, most often because the underlying market has closed.

<Note>
  On devnet, every market runs in **`ProxyMode`** all the time, by design. `ProxyMode` means the program prices off the keeper-pushed `user_twap_price` instead of reading a Pyth account. `Normal` is the mainnet steady state, where mint and redeem read Pyth directly.
</Note>

## The states

| State | Meaning | User mint | User redeem | Price used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Normal` | Pyth-direct steady state (mainnet) | Open | Open | Pyth account, at most 60 s old |
| `ProxyMode` | Pricing off the keeper-pushed price (devnet) | Open | Open | `user_twap_price`, at most 300 s old |
| `Stress` | Oracle unreliable, defensive | Blocked by default (`MintNotAllowedInStress`) | Blocked by default (`RedeemNotAllowedInStress`) | `user_twap_price` |
| `Recovery` | Wind-down after `Stress` | Open | Open | `user_twap_price`, at most 300 s old |

`Stress` blocks both sides because `allow_mint_in_stress` and `allow_redeem_in_stress` default to false, and both are false on all markets, 14 when checked on 10 Oct 2026 and the ten live markets now. The market authority can turn either on with `configure_risk_fees`. A mint in `Stress` is then also held to `stress_mint_cap` per transaction. That cap is 0 (no cap) on every market.

## Fee and price band

The fee is the market's posted fee in every state. Since 20 Nov 2026 both fee multipliers (`proxy_fee_multiplier` and `stress_fee_multiplier`) are 1× on every live market, so a posted 18 bps is charged as 18 bps. Before, in `ProxyMode`, it was charged as 27. The market authority can change them with `configure_risk_fees`.

The price band `c` that mint and redeem quote against does not depend on the state either. The band rule is set on all ten live markets, and under it `c` grows with the age of the price:

```
c = (sigmas_x100 / 100) × oracle_confidence × sqrt(age / horizon_secs)
```

`age` is the chain time now minus the price's own stamp, never below 0. The stamp is `twap_updated_at` for the keeper's stored price, or the publish time of the oracle print when the program reads the oracle itself in `Normal`. `oracle_confidence` is read as one standard deviation of the price over `horizon_secs`. On devnet it is the keeper's GJR-GARCH forecast for a 30-second bar. The market authority sets `sigmas_x100` and `horizon_secs` with `configure_price_band`. All ten live markets use 2 standard deviations over 30 seconds. The band applies only where worst-case quoting is on (`worst_case_config.enabled`) and a confidence is posted. Quoting is on for all ten live markets (QQQ's was switched on on 21 Nov 2026; until then QQQ quoted with no band).

A price read the second it was stamped has no band. At one horizon the band is `sigmas` standard deviations. At four horizons it is twice that. With 2 standard deviations over 30 s:

| Price age | Band as a multiple of `oracle_confidence` |
|---|---|
| 15 s | 1.414× |
| 30 s | 2× |
| 60 s | 2.83× |
| 120 s | 4× |
| 300 s | 6.32× |

Why an age rule: a replay of ten weekdays of the keeper's own prices found that the move while a price stood grew with the square root of the time it stood. At the same average width, the age rule covered 86% of those moves and the flat 1.5× band covered 73%. At 2 standard deviations it covers 91%. Currency and metal prices were about 52 s old when used, and stock prices about 16 s, measured while the currency markets were live.

If a market's band rule is off (`band_sigmas_x100` and `band_horizon_secs` both 0), the old rule applies: `oracle_confidence` times the multiplier in `worst_case_config`, set with `configure_worst_case_quoting`. No live market is in that case. The formulas are on [Mint](/flows/mint#math) and in [NAV math](/concepts/nav#worst-case-quoting).

`Recovery` now quotes exactly like `ProxyMode`. It stays a separate state, and the keeper still holds it for an hour after leaving `Stress`.

### Transition rules

The on-chain transition matrix stops a compromised keeper from skipping recovery: **`Stress` can only move to `Recovery`** (or stay in `Stress`). A direct `Stress` → `Normal` or `Stress` → `ProxyMode` jump is rejected. The rule is unchanged, but it no longer protects a surcharge, because the fee multipliers are 1× and the band ignores the state. Every other transition is allowed: `ProxyMode` and `Recovery` move freely, and `Normal` can be entered from any state except `Stress`. Each push is also capped on price (a 25% move limit); see [Oracle](/concepts/oracle#what-one-push-contains).

## The staleness chain (off-hours behavior)

Risk states describe the oracle's *quality*. A separate chain handles its *absence*, most often when the underlying market closes for the night or the weekend:

```
underlying closes
  → source publish time > 120 s old      keeper withholds the push
  → twap_updated_at > 300 s old          MAX_TWAP_AGE_SECS gate trips
  → mint and redeem revert               TwapNotAvailable
  → books switch to free-float books     off-hours price discovery
```

The program enforces the freeze, but only the keeper's silence causes it. Any accepted push resets `twap_updated_at`, so the freeze lasts exactly as long as the keeper withholds. While frozen, mint and redeem revert in every state except `Normal`, the bin books, which fill only while the price is stale outside `Normal`, trade against free-float books, and committed orders can't settle; the keeper refunds each one about 5 s after its 180 s TTL. The first accepted push at reopen re-anchors NAV. The full chain is on [Oracle](/concepts/oracle#the-staleness-chain-what-happens-when-the-market-closes).

If you only check one thing before you submit a mint or redeem, check `market.twap_updated_at`.

## What you should do in each state

### `ProxyMode` (devnet)

Operate normally. The fee is the posted fee, and the band on both legs grows with the age of the price. If you run a bot, remember that a mint or redeem needs a push no more than 300 s old.

### `Stress`

Mint and redeem are blocked by default (`MintNotAllowedInStress`, `RedeemNotAllowedInStress`). Committed orders still fill while the keeper pushes prices, and the bin books open once the price is more than 300 s old. If you build a UI, say so:

> "QQQ is in Stress mode. Minting and redeeming are paused; you can still trade QQQL and QQQS."

### `Recovery`

Mint and redeem reopen. They quote exactly as in `ProxyMode`.

## Reading risk state

```typescript
const market = await mintRedeem.account.market.fetch(marketPDA);

// market.riskState is an enum object: { normal: {} } | { proxyMode: {} } | ...
const riskState = Object.keys(market.riskState)[0];
const priceAge = Date.now() / 1000 - market.twapUpdatedAt.toNumber();

console.log("Risk state:", riskState);
console.log("Price age (s):", priceAge.toFixed(0), priceAge > 300 ? "(frozen)" : "(fresh)");
console.log("Posted fees (bps):", market.mintFeeBps, "/", market.redeemFeeBps);
```

The risk state can lag reality by one keeper tick. Outside `Normal`, the price age is the authoritative "will my transaction go through" signal.

## State during maintenance

For a hard halt, the market authority sets `market.is_active = false` with `update_market`. Mint and redeem then revert (`MarketNotActive`), and so does `commit_swap` (`MarketInactive`). `pool_swap` doesn't check the flag, so the books still fill while the price is stale, and `settle_swap` refunds the orders of a paused market; after the TTL anyone can send `cancel_swap` to refund an order, whatever the state.
