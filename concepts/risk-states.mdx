title: Risk states
description: The per-market risk regime, the fee and confidence surcharges each state applies, why devnet runs in ProxyMode, and the staleness gate that freezes mint and redeem off-hours.
---

Every market carries a `RiskState` on its [`Market`](/programs/mint-redeem#market-account) account. The keeper writes it with each price push (`update_risk_state_v2`). The state sets two surcharges, one on the fee and one on the confidence band, and decides whether mint and redeem are open at all. A separate staleness gate freezes mint and redeem when pushes stop, most often because the underlying market has closed.

<Note>
  On devnet, every market runs in **`ProxyMode`** all the time, by design. `ProxyMode` means the program prices off the keeper-pushed `user_twap_price` instead of reading a Pyth account. `Normal` is the mainnet steady state, where mint and redeem read Pyth directly.
</Note>

## The states

| State | Meaning | User mint | User redeem | Price used |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Normal` | Pyth-direct steady state (mainnet) | Open | Open | Pyth account, at most 60 s old |
| `ProxyMode` | Pricing off the keeper-pushed price (devnet) | Open | Open | `user_twap_price`, at most 300 s old |
| `Stress` | Oracle unreliable, defensive | Blocked by default (`MintNotAllowedInStress`) | Blocked by default (`RedeemNotAllowedInStress`) | `user_twap_price` |
| `Recovery` | Wind-down after `Stress` | Open | Open | `user_twap_price`, at most 300 s old |

`Stress` blocks both sides because `allow_mint_in_stress` and `allow_redeem_in_stress` default to false, and both are false on all markets, 14 when checked on 10 Oct 2026 and 13 live now. The market authority can turn either on with `configure_risk_fees`. A mint in `Stress` is then also held to `stress_mint_cap` per transaction. That cap is 0 (no cap) on every market.

## Fee and confidence multipliers

Each state multiplies the market's posted fee and its confidence band. Both multipliers are surcharges on top of the posted values, and every live setting is 1× or more.

| State | Fee multiplier | Confidence multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| `Normal` | 1× | 1× |
| `ProxyMode` | 1.5× (`proxy_fee_multiplier`) | 1.5× (`proxy_confidence_multiplier`) |
| `Stress` | 3× (`stress_fee_multiplier`), if trading is allowed | 3× (`stress_confidence_multiplier`) |
| `Recovery` | Falls from 3× to 1× over 1 hour | Falls from 3× to 1× over 1 hour |

These are the defaults, and the values on every live market as of 10 Oct 2026. The market authority sets the fee multipliers with `configure_risk_fees` and the confidence multipliers with `configure_worst_case_quoting`. The confidence multiplier applies only where worst-case quoting is on; see [NAV math](/concepts/nav#worst-case-quoting).

On devnet the 1.5× fee multiplier always applies: QQQ posts an 18 bps fee and is charged 27 bps.

The `Recovery` decline is linear and starts when the market enters `Recovery`. The program sets `risk_state_updated_at` only when the state changes, so later pushes in `Recovery` don't restart the clock. The keeper holds `Recovery` for an hour after leaving `Stress`, so the surcharge is back to 1× by the time the keeper moves the market to its next state.

### Transition rules

The on-chain transition matrix stops a compromised keeper from skipping recovery: **`Stress` can only move to `Recovery`** (or stay in `Stress`). A direct `Stress` → `Normal` or `Stress` → `ProxyMode` jump is rejected, which would otherwise drop the surcharge from 3× straight away. Every other transition is allowed: `ProxyMode` and `Recovery` move freely, and `Normal` can be entered from any state except `Stress`. Each push is also capped on price (a 25% move limit); see [Oracle](/concepts/oracle#what-one-push-contains).

## The staleness chain (off-hours behavior)

Risk states describe the oracle's *quality*. A separate chain handles its *absence*, most often when the underlying market closes for the night or the weekend:

```
underlying closes
  → source publish time > 120 s old      keeper withholds the push
  → twap_updated_at > 300 s old          MAX_TWAP_AGE_SECS gate trips
  → mint and redeem revert               TwapNotAvailable
  → books switch to free-float books     off-hours price discovery
```

The program enforces the freeze, but only the keeper's silence causes it. Any accepted push resets `twap_updated_at`, so the freeze lasts exactly as long as the keeper withholds. While frozen, mint and redeem revert in every state except `Normal`, the bin books, which fill only while the price is stale outside `Normal`, trade against free-float books, and committed orders can't settle; the keeper refunds each one about 5 s after its 180 s TTL. The first accepted push at reopen re-anchors NAV. The full chain is on [Oracle](/concepts/oracle#the-staleness-chain-what-happens-when-the-market-closes).

If you only check one thing before you submit a mint or redeem, check `market.twap_updated_at`.

## What you should do in each state

### `ProxyMode` (devnet)

Operate normally. Fees carry the 1.5× surcharge, and quotes carry a 1.5× confidence markup on both legs, usually a few bps. If you run a bot, remember that a mint or redeem needs a push no more than 300 s old.

### `Stress`

Mint and redeem are blocked by default (`MintNotAllowedInStress`, `RedeemNotAllowedInStress`). Committed orders still fill while the keeper pushes prices, and the bin books open once the price is more than 300 s old. If you build a UI, say so:

> "QQQ is in Stress mode. Minting and redeeming are paused; you can still trade QQQL and QQQS."

### `Recovery`

Mint and redeem reopen. Fees and confidence markups start at 3× and fall evenly to 1× over the first hour.

## Reading risk state

```typescript
const market = await mintRedeem.account.market.fetch(marketPDA);

// market.riskState is an enum object: { normal: {} } | { proxyMode: {} } | ...
const riskState = Object.keys(market.riskState)[0];
const priceAge = Date.now() / 1000 - market.twapUpdatedAt.toNumber();

console.log("Risk state:", riskState);
console.log("Price age (s):", priceAge.toFixed(0), priceAge > 300 ? "(frozen)" : "(fresh)");
console.log("Posted fees (bps):", market.mintFeeBps, "/", market.redeemFeeBps);
```

The risk state can lag reality by one keeper tick. Outside `Normal`, the price age is the authoritative "will my transaction go through" signal.

## State during maintenance

For a hard halt, the market authority sets `market.is_active = false` with `update_market`. Mint and redeem then revert (`MarketNotActive`), and so does `commit_swap` (`MarketInactive`). `pool_swap` doesn't check the flag, so the books still fill while the price is stale, and `settle_swap` refunds the orders of a paused market; after the TTL anyone can send `cancel_swap` to refund an order, whatever the state.
