title: CLP - Continuous Liquidity Provider
description: The on-chain program that hosts Continuum's trading venues and the vaults that stock them with inventory.
---

The **CLP** (Continuous Liquidity Provider) is the on-chain program where all trading happens. It hosts two venues, committed orders while a market is open and native bin books while its price is stale, plus the per-market vaults that stock them with inventory. The instant oracle swap it also hosted was retired on 11 Oct 2026.

Users call the CLP every time they trade a leg. Inventory and treasury operations, such as funding books, sweeping excess and minting pairs into vaults, are gated to the keeper or an admin.

## Venue 1: native bin books

Each market has one `Pool` account **per side**, a long pool and a short pool (PDA seeds `[b"pool", market, side]`), so there are 26 native pools across the 13 live markets.

- Up to 16 price bins live inline in the `Pool` account itself (live books use 12, and XAU's long book uses 6). There are no separate bin-array accounts and no rent churn on a reposition.
- The geometry has two zones: fine core steps near the centre and wide wings further out. QQQ uses three 20 bps core steps, then 300 bps wings. `configure_pool_geom(wing_step_bps, core_bins)` sets it.
- The keeper posts the shape. `set_pool_shape(base_price_1e6, asks, bids)` writes the centre and the per-bin sizes in one call, and the program checks the sums against the pool's vault balances. The keeper centres each book on NAV plus a small inventory shift, and repositions it when NAV drifts past a gate of at most 20 bps.

`pool_swap(params: PoolSwapParams { direction, amount_in, min_out })` walks the bins with real slippage and charges no swap fee. It is pure inventory trading: nothing is minted or burned. It trades only while the market floats: its risk state isn't `Normal` and its last price push is more than 300 s old, the same condition that freezes mint and redeem. While prices are pushing it fails with `PoolClosedWhileLive`, and trades go through committed orders. Nothing in the program can set `pool.frozen` or disable a pool, so there is no pool-level kill switch. `pool_swap` is held to a rolling notional cap: 1,000,000 cUSDC per hour on QQQ, SPY and XAU, and unset (uncapped) on the other markets, as of 11 Oct 2026.

### What the book actually is

The keeper's env vars carry a `CLMM_` prefix and its pricing module is `clmm.rs`. Those are legacy names. The venue is a bin book, and it works differently from a Uniswap v3 pool:

| | Uniswap v3 pool | Continuum's bin book |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity shape | Continuous `x·y=k` curve inside a tick range | Discrete bins; a fixed reserve at a fixed price |
| Price inside a unit | Moves continuously along the curve | Constant within a bin; a fill walks to the next bin |
| Who supplies it | Many permissionless LPs choosing their own ranges | One quoter: the protocol is the sole LP |
| How the shape is set | Emerges from LP deposits | Posted by the keeper (`set_pool_shape`) at each reposition |

Mechanically it is a **discrete-bin liquidity book**, the same family as Trader Joe's Liquidity Book and Meteora's DLMM (the "D" is for *discrete*), which is what the program's `clmm.rs` header means by "DLMM-style". Ask bin `i` sits `i + 1` steps above the centre and bid bin `i` sits `i + 1` steps below. Each step multiplies the price by `1 + step/1e4`, using the core step for the first `core_bins` steps and the wing step beyond. A bin holds a reserve and fills at its own price until it runs out.

It differs from those too, by being *less* of an AMM. The whole book lives inline in one `Pool` account with no per-bin accounts, and the keeper **posts** the ladder; deposits don't shape it. There is no bonding curve. The most accurate description is a **keeper-quoted bin book**: an on-chain market maker whose quotes are settled by an AMM-style swap instruction.

We keep the `CLMM_*` env-var prefix and the `clmm.rs` filename for compatibility with deployed configuration; the docs say "book" or "bin book".

### Lean by design

Book depth is sized to **demand, never to TVL**:

- Loss to toxic arbitrage grows with deployed depth, while organic revenue is capped by demand, so depth beyond demand is pure downside.
- Bid cUSDC sitting in bins earns nothing, so bids are capped at about \$5k per side. The excess is swept to the CLP vault, where it provisions committed orders.
- Asks trim to at most 1.5× bids via `pool_sweep_synth`.

The keeper's refill loop works both ways. Drained asks restock by sweeping bids, minting pairs with `vault_mint_pairs` and funding both sides; bloated sides trim back. Large sells don't need standing bids at all: while the market is open they go through committed orders, and the keeper provisions the cUSDC at settle time, from the treasury or by redeeming pairs.

## Venue 2: committed orders

The large-order path uses three instructions and one account:

```
commit_swap(side, direction, amount_in, min_out, nonce)
    → escrows the input into an Order PDA [b"order", user, market, nonce]
      (TTL 180 s)
settle_swap()                   ── permissionless
    → fills at NAV ± max(commit_spread_bps, 10) bps at the latest push,
      which must be newer than the commit; min_out miss → full refund
cancel_swap()                   ── anyone, after the TTL
    → refund to the owner; checks nothing else
```

Since 11 Oct 2026, committed orders are the protocol's only venue while a market is open, and they run on every live market. `commit_swap` needs the market's swap guards (`swap_guards`) enabled and not frozen.

How the venue behaves:

- There is no size cap. The fill price is set after the commitment, so latency arbitrage is impossible by construction. The limits are oracle health, a 100% collateral floor and keeper-provisioned inventory. A fill that fails any of them reverts, and the order stays pending for a later push. A `min_out` miss refunds the order instead.
- `COMMIT_SPREAD_BPS = 10` is only a floor. The keeper posts `commit_spread_bps = clamp(half-spread, 10, 500)` from the books' vol model: 18 bps for QQQ, 15 for SPY and 18 for XAU as of 10 Oct 2026. The post-commit price removes latency arbitrage, and the spread prices the protocol's convexity exposure on large fills.
- Funds are always recoverable. After the 180 s TTL (`COMMIT_TTL_SECS`), anyone can send `cancel_swap`, which returns the escrow to the owner whatever the oracle, keeper or admin state. The keeper sends it about 5 s after expiry, and the app offers a **Refund now** button 60 s after. Before expiry nobody can cancel. Every terminal path (settle, refund, cancel) closes the `Order` account and returns its rent.
- Anyone can call `settle_swap`. The keeper cranks it every 20 s, or every 5 s while orders are pending.

The `Order` account layout supports `getProgramAccounts` filters: `user` at offset 8, `market` at 40, `status` at 74.

<Warning>
  `settle_swap` fills at the keeper-written `user_twap_price`, and its freshness check uses the keeper-written `twap_updated_at`. Both come from one key; nothing on chain checks them against an independent price.
</Warning>

## Retired: the instant oracle swap

`clp_swap(side, direction, amount_in, min_out)` filled instantly from CLP inventory at NAV ± `spread_bps`, on QQQ, SPY and XAU. It filled at the last pushed price, which is a stale quote between pushes, and it was retired on 11 Oct 2026. The instruction keeps its accounts and arguments, so existing clients still decode it, but every call fails with `SwapDisabled`.

Its guards, set by `configure_swap`, now serve committed orders. On the 13 markets with guards on, `settle_swap` needs a price at most 600 s old and a market at least 100% collateralized at that price. The confidence and deviation limits (5,000 and 10,000 bps) are off in practice, and the price write itself refuses a move of more than 25% per push.

## Vaults and capital flow

```
Admin funding (GlobalClp → allocate_to_market)
    ▼
Per-market CLP vault (cUSDC)
    │ vault_mint_pairs        → CLP-PDA-signed CPI into mint-redeem;
    ▼                            paired L+S land in CLP custody
CLP inventory (L + S + cUSDC)
    │ clp_fund_pool_synth /   → stock the per-side books
    │ set_pool_shape
    ▼
bin books ◄──── pool_sweep_usdc / pool_sweep_synth ──── excess back to the CLP
    │
    └─ committed orders provision at settle time, from the vault
       or the treasury (vault_mint_pairs / vault_redeem_pairs as needed)
```

`vault_mint_pairs`, `vault_redeem_pairs` and `vault_redeem_single` are CPIs into mint-redeem signed by the CLP PDA. The paired ones go through the ordinary `mint_paired` and `redeem_paired`, at NAV and with the same fee and price checks as any user. Each one must name the CLP's own market, and its cUSDC, L and S accounts must belong to the CLP PDA. Inventory can't be minted against another market or paid to a wallet.

Each market keeps its collateral in its own vault, owned by its Market PDA, and keeps its own `total_collateral` ledger. Every outflow is limited to that market's liquid share, `total_collateral − deployed_to_yield`. QQQ, SPY and XAU shared a unified vault until 11 Oct 2026. After they moved out, every vault's balance equaled its market's ledger, and the program upgrade that day removed the unified vault. A cross-market rebalancer moves only collateral above the source market's own posted buffer target. `rebalance_collateral` on mint-redeem caps each move at that amount on-chain, so a larger request moves less and never takes the source below target.

## The treasury

The `GlobalClp` vault at the top of the diagram is the protocol treasury. It held about 40.0M cUSDC late on 12 Oct 2026, after the keeper returned idle CLP cash on 11 Oct and the CLP's own holdings were cut to working size on 12 Oct. Besides `allocate_to_market`, three instructions draw on it:

- `backstop_market` moves cUSDC straight into a market's collateral vault and credits its `total_collateral`. It has no rate limit, because the funds stay inside the protocol. The keeper calls it when a market falls below 100% or stays short of its posted target; see [Solvency → Treasury backstop](/concepts/solvency#treasury-backstop).
- `admin_withdraw` pays cUSDC out of the protocol, to one named account only. Its allowance refills at a rate the keeper keeps in proportion to the balance, so a full exit takes 24 h whatever the treasury holds. Loosening the limits waits 24 h. A guardian key can freeze withdrawals and veto a loosening, but it can't move funds.
- `treasury_lend` sends idle cUSDC to a lending venue and `treasury_recall` brings it back. A lend spends the same allowance as `admin_withdraw` and stops while the treasury is frozen; a recall restores the allowance and always works. Nothing lends the treasury on a schedule yet, and devnet has no venues.

The keeper's yield allocator counts the treasury as first-loss capital when it sizes lending positions. See [clp → Treasury](/programs/clp#treasury) for the instructions.

## Where protocol revenue accumulates

- Trading-spread profits build up in the pool vaults and CLP custody as the books buy below NAV and sell above it. They reach a market's collateral only when someone adds them. Anyone can call `donate_to_vault`, and the market authority can call `fund_collateral`. Profits deposited into the treasury reach a market through `backstop_market`, which the keeper calls when a market falls below 100% or stays short of its target. The keeper calls neither `donate_to_vault` nor `fund_collateral`.
- Of each mint and redeem fee, 90% stays in the market's collateral vault and 10% (`dev_tax_pct`) goes to the market's fee recipient, which has been the treasury on every live market since 12 Oct 2026.
- Harvested yield goes into the market's own vault and is added to `total_collateral`, so it raises that market's collateral ratio. Collateral above the target can still move to the CLP with `withdraw_excess_to_clp`. Nothing is lent on devnet: the keeper's emulator harvests the yield its mainnet plan would earn, and every live market has `yield_enabled` set as of 11 Oct 2026.

See [Solvency](/concepts/solvency) for how these channels affect the collateral ratio.

## Reading pool state

```typescript
import { web3 } from "@coral-xyz/anchor";

const side = 0; // 0 = long, 1 = short
const [poolPDA] = web3.PublicKey.findProgramAddressSync(
  [Buffer.from("pool"), marketPDA.toBuffer(), Buffer.from([side])],
  clpProgram.programId,
);
const pool = await clpProgram.account.pool.fetch(poolPDA);
console.log("Centre price:", pool.basePrice1E6.toString());
console.log("Bins:", pool.numBins, "core step:", pool.stepBps, "x", pool.coreBins, "wing step:", pool.wingStepBps);
```

The app's [/pools](https://continuum.markets/pools) page renders this directly: the per-leg pool price, a deviation-from-NAV badge, instant depth and the per-market spread.
