# Continuum UX audit — 10 September 2026

## Scope and verdict

Reviewed the complete live homepage at https://continuum.markets/, plus the public `/pools` and `/mint` entry journey. This is not an exhaustive audit of every application route, guide, comparison article, or transaction flow.

The visual identity is coherent: dark ground, violet accents, editorial serif headings, and protocol illustrations. Keep it. The main problem is that mobile preserves the desktop presentation's scroll length while removing context or shrinking content. The screenshots identify real layout issues, not a need for more decoration.

**Screen job:** help a prospective user understand what synthetic exposure they can try, what is available now, why to trust the mechanism, and how to take the next step.

**Visual target:** retain the current identity, but make every settled mobile section answer a question with readable text, artwork, and an obvious continuation. Treat this as a marketing page, not a trading dashboard. No new component library or decorative effect is recommended.

## Evidence and coverage

- Browser: hosted Chrome, using an isolated context. Local Chrome DevTools was unavailable because its profile was already in use. An existing stopped browser was reused without accessing its unrelated account state.
- Viewports: 375 × 760 and 393 × 656 (mobile/touch emulation), 768 × 1024 (tablet-sized, touch enabled), 1440 × 900 (desktop).
- Inspected screenshots of the hero, market-access graph, stack, market-structure section, comparison, final CTA/footer, and app entry screens. Not every section was screenshotted at every size.
- Exercised all four mobile stack stops, large scroll jumps, reverse jumps, a synthetic desktop wheel event, mobile menu open/close and Escape, homepage-to-markets navigation, disconnected mint state, and wallet-dialog open/close. No wallet was connected; no transaction was signed or submitted.
- Checked live geometry and local source. Source references below are relative to `frontend/`; they are not proof of a particular deployed commit.
- Lighthouse desktop snapshot: accessibility 94, best practices 100, SEO 100. Failures: `color-contrast` on Launch App and `heading-order` on Equities. These scores do not validate the visual design.
- One homepage reload trace at 375 × 760, 4× CPU slowdown and Fast 4G: LCP 406 ms, CLS 0.00. This reused a previously loaded browser context; it is not a cold-cache or real-device benchmark. No CrUX field data was returned.

## Highest-priority findings

### 1. Stack: the diagram overlaps its description on shorter phones

**Priority: P1 — reading obstruction. Browser-confirmed, source-explained.**

At 393 × 656, step 03's SVG occupies y=92–385.83, while its card starts at y=328.16: the boxes overlap by **57.67 px**. The bottom block visibly covers the card's step-label area. At 375 × 760 the SVG ends at y=371.17 and the card starts at y=441.59, leaving **70.42 px**. This explains why testing one tall phone can miss the defect.

Cause: `--stack-diagram-h` changes from 42svh to 32svh below 700px viewport height, then 26svh below 620px. Card padding and the sticky wrapper use this reservation, but the inner SVG remains width-driven with a viewBox ratio of 860:700. Reducing the reservation does not shrink the SVG. The comment that these dimensions cannot drift does not match the rendered result.

Relevant source:
- `app/globals.css:317–335`
- `components/landing/continuum-stack-scene.tsx:235–238, 580–627`

**Recommended change:** use normal document flow for the mobile diagram and descriptions, or constrain the artwork's actual dimensions to the same box used for spacing. Prefer a compact diagram with a readable numbered explanation rather than four mandatory full-screen cards. Do not solve the overlap by making already-small SVG labels even smaller. Use normal HTML labels where needed and a deliberate 32–48px diagram-to-copy gap as an initial design target, not a universal constant.

### 2. Market-access graph: mobile removes the explanation but keeps two screens

**Priority: P1 — missing information and wasted scroll. Browser-confirmed, source-explained.**

At 375 × 760, the graph section occupies **1,520 px**, with two full-screen stops. Neither settled stop has a visible heading or description. Desktop has four explanatory callouts; mobile and tablet lose them. At 768px width the graph is capped at 416px wide and still occupies two 1,024px screens.

The mobile SVG labels measure approximately **5.9–6.4 CSS px** after the SVG-to-screen transform at 375px width. A large circle is not the same as a readable label. The second state also leaves a small orphan dot where the stablecoin source disappears.

Cause: `AccessSnapScene` explicitly passes `showMobileCards={false}`, while its two empty `min-h-[100svh]` containers remain. The component already has a mobile card branch, but it is disabled.

Relevant source:
- `app/home-page.tsx:105–106, 212–240`
- `components/landing/market-access-scene.tsx:509–523`

**Recommended change:** make one content-led mobile section: a heading, a compact graph, and four short market descriptions with actual examples. Keep the desktop illustration's identity, not its forced scroll geometry. Simply toggling the cards back on inside the existing sticky full-screen overlay could create new clipping; rebuild the mobile composition in normal flow.

### 3. Market structure/arbitrage: the graphics and explanation do not form a readable unit

**Priority: P1 — hierarchy and comprehension. Browser-confirmed.**

On mobile the layout is an unlabeled red distribution, a centered technical paragraph, then an unlabeled green distribution. The graphics look like data but provide no axis, price marker, or textual relationship explaining what the reader should learn. Their bar lengths are hard-coded presentation values in source, not measured market data.

At 393 × 656, body copy computes to **13.6px** and the heading to **35.424px**. Text size responds to viewport height, reducing readability to preserve the composition. At 768 × 1024, the section is **1,349px tall**: the top graphic takes substantial space and the lower graphic falls below the first viewport. This is an overflow/composition issue; the audit did not establish that the entire lower graphic is unreachable with native touch.

The text also combines different questions: oracle pricing, temporary imbalance, correction, and a $5,000 order rule. The heading “The real price. Every trade.” sounds absolute, while the app describes NAV ± spread and can show stale-price restrictions.

Relevant source:
- `app/home-page.tsx:152–155, 637–658, 825, 881–888, 972–998`

**Recommended change:** put the heading first; show one compact illustration with explicit Long, Short, and reference-price labels; follow with two or three short left-aligned explanations. Separate the large-order rule into a note. If the artwork remains conceptual, label it illustrative rather than suggesting live data. Validate pricing claims against current execution rules, including spread, fees, freshness, and off-hours behavior.

## Site-wide findings

### 4. Devnet status disappears exactly where mobile users need it

**Priority: P1 — expectation and trust.**

Desktop/tablet show “Live on Solana devnet” in the hero's small credential band. Phones hide it using `hidden ... sm:inline-flex`. The hero and header say “Launch App”; the final CTA says “Open the devnet app.” The markets screen shows dollar TVL and projected APR without visible devnet wording in the inspected page text. The wallet dialog eventually discloses Devnet, but only after the visitor chooses to connect.

**Recommended change:** place a plain-language test-network notice beside the first CTA at all sizes and carry it into the app shell and metric context. Use one consistent CTA label. Explain test cUSDC and the faucet before asking someone to connect; do not imply test-network TVL is mainnet adoption proof.

Source: `components/landing/trust-band.tsx:269–281`; `components/Header.tsx:84–90`; `components/landing/final-cta.tsx:383–417`.

### 5. The page leads with protocol mechanics before resolving product questions

**Priority: P1 — comprehension and conversion.**

At 375 × 760 the homepage is 7,600px tall. Six of its ten viewport-height units are devoted to the graph and stack. Mobile readers encounter CLP, NAV, paired L/S tokens, a bin book, and SPL before seeing a simple worked example of what they receive.

The hero communicates asset categories and round-the-clock access, but “Trade like institutions do” does not explain synthetic exposure or distinguish exposure from ownership of the underlying asset. There is no clear early answer to “What can I try today, with which funds?”

**Recommended change:** move the concrete product explanation and current availability earlier. Treat architecture as a secondary explanation, not six screens of prerequisite reading. A useful demonstration is the creation and use of paired tokens, not a promised trading return. Put detailed terms, supported operations, fees, and risks behind clear links rather than deleting them.

### 6. Comparison claims lose their explanation on mobile

**Priority: P2 — evidence and accessibility.**

The comparison shows competitor logos with “Needs trust” / “Not portable.” The explanatory tooltip is `hidden ... sm:block` and uses `group-hover:opacity-100`; it is absent on phones and not a keyboard-operated disclosure. The visible comparison does not explain Continuum's own dependencies or provide an evidence trail.

The final CTA says “you can borrow against the position,” while stack step 04 more carefully says a lending market “can list” the SPL tokens. The audit did not verify a working borrow route or an external listing. A capability should not be advertised as currently available without supporting evidence.

**Recommended change:** give competitors visible names and concise, sourced differences; keep explanations available on tap and keyboard. State Continuum's tradeoffs and current availability. Remove or qualify the borrowing claim until a supported route/listing can be demonstrated. Keep “Built on” separate from endorsement or security proof.

Source: `app/home-page.tsx:107–149, 462–471`; `components/landing/continuum-stack-scene.tsx:25–36`; `components/landing/final-cta.tsx:364`.

### 7. Navigation prioritizes presentation over control

**Priority: P2 — navigation and recovery.**

- The phone menu hides only two destinations: Docs and Launch App. After the hero, the main action requires opening this menu; there are no section shortcuts through the long presentation.
- Escape left the mobile menu expanded. Clicking the toggle again closed it.
- Desktop Page Down with `BODY` focused did not advance the nested landing scroller (scrollTop stayed 7160); the scroller has no explicit tabindex. A synthetic wheel event did advance from 7160 to 8060. That is not proof of real trackpad behavior.
- At 393 × 656, the fixed header masks part of the final CTA's eyebrow at the last settled stop.
- At desktop width, the stack timeline sits at the extreme left edge, unlike the inset header and final CTA; it breaks the page's otherwise deliberate alignment.

**Recommended change:** keep a compact primary action visible on mobile, or simplify the two-item navigation. Make the actual scroll region keyboard-operable. If chapters remain, provide useful destinations and an exit rather than enforcing a stop for sparse artwork. Preserve browser history/focus for anchors. Use one header-safe content boundary and a consistent desktop content grid.

### 8. Contrast, semantics, and targets need a focused accessibility pass

**Priority: P2 — independently measured.**

- Launch App uses white text on `rgb(111,107,255)` at 14px. Calculated contrast: **4.02:1**, below the 4.5:1 normal-text threshold. Lighthouse flagged it.
- Lighthouse flagged heading order at the Equities h3. Give the market-access and stack sections proper h2 parents.
- The mobile menu toggle is **40 × 40px**; footer social links are **24 × 24px**. Increase touch hit areas toward 44px without necessarily enlarging the icons. These sizes are design recommendations, not a blanket claim that every sub-44px target violates WCAG.
- The diagram labels are substantially smaller than their SVG fontSize attributes suggest. Measure transformed text, not just declared font sizes.

**Recommended change:** adjust the existing CTA fill/text relationship to meet AA, make section semantics complete, expand targets, and verify focus visibility and text at larger sizes. Do not use the accessibility score as a design verdict.

### 9. Mint entry has a disabled action named “Connect Wallet”

**Priority: P1 for the app entry journey. Browser-confirmed, source-explained.**

In the disconnected state, the form's “Connect Wallet” button is disabled. The header's “Select Wallet” does open a correctly named dialog, and Escape closes that dialog. Users must discover the separate header control because the obvious form action cannot work.

Source confirms the form disables its button when `!connected` and labels it “Connect Wallet” in that same state. This is not caused by the stale-price warning: it is independently disabled by connection state.

During the audit, QQQ also displayed an on-chain price roughly 266 minutes old and said the program would reject, while another notice said treasury liquidity remained active for off-hours trading. These may describe different operations, but the form does not clearly explain that distinction to a new visitor. This was a point-in-time observation, not a persistent availability diagnosis.

**Recommended change:** make the disconnected form action open the wallet picker. Separately gate the transaction action on a valid quote and explain which operation is unavailable and how recovery happens. Preserve entered values. Keep technical keeper language secondary to an actionable status.

Source: `components/MintRedeemView.tsx:543, 565–568`.

## Business-library application

The following are source-grounded principles, followed by recommendations for this site—not claims that the books prescribe a particular layout or financial outcome.

1. **Alex Hormozi, _\$100M Offers_ → “Value Offer: The Value Equation.”** The section distinguishes desired outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, and effort/sacrifice. Continuum spends substantial space on aspiration and mechanism, but leaves uncertainty about network, funding, what the tokens represent, and what works now. Reduce actual onboarding effort and make these facts visible before the CTA. Do not replace uncertainty with stronger promises.
2. **Alex Hormozi, _Proof Checklist_ → “Belief Continuum” / “How I Make Proof More Compelling.”** The source emphasizes evidence and demonstrations over unsupported promises. Use verifiable product proof: a labeled devnet walkthrough, an explorer-linked example, documented execution constraints, and any actual published audit with its scope/date. Do not invent audit status, adoption metrics, or integration availability.
3. **Alex Hormozi, _\$100M Leads_ → “Section I: Start Here” → “Engage Your Leads: Offers and Lead Magnets,” Step 7.** CTAs should say clearly what to do next. Use a consistent test-app action and explain the next step. There is no need to import artificial urgency or scarcity into a financial product.

## Four UX principles that change the recommendation

- **Proximity:** keep diagrams and their explanation together, with an actual shared layout rather than separate layers whose reservations can drift.
- **Miller's Law:** split the market-structure paragraph into understandable chunks and avoid requiring readers to retain jargon across multiple screens.
- **Jakob's Law:** preserve familiar scrolling and keyboard input; expose ordinary navigation rather than making a custom presentation the only path.
- **Von Restorff:** keep the primary action distinct and visible while reducing competing decorative demands. A polished illustration should support, not replace, the message.

Apple's [HIG — Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views), inspected in the browser, specifically supports familiar gestures/keyboard shortcuts, discoverable overflow, and paging when the content warrants it. It does not prescribe Continuum's custom wheel controller or require full-screen marketing chapters.

## Recommended implementation order

1. Repair mobile stack geometry, restore the market explanations, and recompose the arbitrage section. These are the user's primary visual failures.
2. Make devnet/funding status and CTA wording consistent; repair the disconnected mint action.
3. Reduce mobile full-screen stops, preserve header clearance, and improve keyboard/menu behavior.
4. Replace unsupported implications with demonstrable product facts; make comparison explanations accessible.
5. Correct CTA contrast, diagram text sizing, heading order, and hit areas.

Acceptance checks: phone portrait at both tested heights, a short landscape viewport, 768px tablet, 1440px desktop, enlarged text, direct entry and reverse navigation, actual touch/trackpad input, and reduced motion before load and toggled during the session. No diagram/card intersection; all copy reachable; no empty mobile chapter; network status visible before connection; every Connect Wallet action works; artwork is either explanatory or explicitly illustrative.

## Original audit limits (before implementation)

- No application code or styling was changed, built, committed, or deployed. This report is the only workspace change.
- No transaction, wallet connection, fund movement, or state-changing backend test was performed. Success states requiring a transaction were not exercised.
- Native iOS Safari, physical touch momentum, pinch zoom, real trackpad momentum, browser-toolbar expansion, and enlarged-text behavior remain unverified. Chrome viewport emulation is not an iPhone Safari test.
- Reduced-motion CSS for text primitives and matchMedia handling in DarkVeil/MagicRings were inspected in source. The current browser tools did not expose a native reduced-motion override, so the rendered fallback was not certified. Direct Framer-driven stack/access movement deserves a separate reduced-motion test.
- No heap/leak study or complete scroll-performance trace was performed. A short recording of the comparison captured ongoing ring movement and hit its frame bound; it did not establish a completed transition duration.
- No exhaustive review of all guides, legal pages, comparison routes, external Docs, market-detail screens, or authenticated application states was performed. No unavailable state was treated as a pass.
- New gallery research was not necessary for this existing-interface audit; recommendations preserve the supplied visual direction rather than proposing a redesign based on uninspected references.

## Implementation follow-up — 10 September 2026

The user approved implementation after this audit. Local frontend changes are complete; they have not been committed or deployed.

### Changes made

- Mobile market access is now an in-flow section with a heading, a settled numbered diagram with directly readable 14px market names, four descriptions, and an explicit distinction between synthetic exposure and underlying ownership. The two empty mobile snap stops are gone.
- The mobile stack uses a simplified version of the same isometric blocks and an in-flow numbered explanation. Its SVG and descriptions no longer depend on unrelated viewport-height reservations. NAV is expanded in the copy; the desktop timeline is inset from the viewport edge.
- Mobile/tablet pricing puts the heading first, labels Long/Short illustrations, identifies them as non-live data, and separates the large-order rule. Body text is 16px rather than height-scaled. The desktop scene is retained with labels and an illustration notice. Decorative distribution animation plays once, not forever.
- Native scrolling replaces the chapter controller on mobile and short viewports. Reduced motion selects the same complete in-flow layout; MotionConfig also respects the user preference. A skip link and keyboard-focusable scroll region are present, with body-focused paging forwarded to that region.
- Header, hero, ending, and analytics use a shared network-aware CTA label. Devnet context appears before the hero action at phone sizes. The app has a test-network notice, faucet link, and compact mobile Devnet label; the longer notice is outside the sticky header.
- The mobile header keeps the main action visible. Escape closes the menu and returns focus. Footer targets and the menu toggle are 44px high; primary-button contrast is corrected using existing brand colors.
- Competitor explanations are native, keyboard/touch-operated disclosures with visible names, plus a link to the comparison pages. The copy acknowledges oracle and smart-contract risks. Unsupported current borrowing claims were replaced with conditional integration language.
- Disconnected Mint and Redeem actions now open the existing wallet picker. Existing connected transaction handlers, eligibility checks, price rules, and backend behavior were not changed. Stale-price wording distinguishes minting from off-hours pool trading.
