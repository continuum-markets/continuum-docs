title: Keeper overview
description: The protocol's off-chain operator. It pushes prices, runs the bin books, settles committed orders and posts per-market parameters.

The keeper is an off-chain Rust service that the protocol runs. It pushes a reference price for each market, manages the 28 bin books, settles committed orders, moves inventory, and posts per-market parameters such as fees and the γ index. Its source code and binary are not published; these pages describe how the protocol's keeper behaves and how it is configured.

Most loops have an environment switch, but not all. γ posting has no switch of its own and stops only if the whole book manager is turned off. Lending collateral, which happens only on mainnet, and the legacy Meteora layer, with its arbitrage loop, ship turned off. See [Keeper configuration](/keeper/running) for variables and defaults.

## What it runs

| Task | Cadence | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Price push** | 5 s in the deployed image (`UPDATE_INTERVAL_MS`, code default 15 s) | Fetches each market's reference price, checks it against Hyperliquid, and writes it with mint-redeem `update_risk_state_v2` to `Market.user_twap_price` (a spot price despite the name, 1e6, corporate-action adjusted) and `twap_updated_at`. If the source price is over 120 s old or the fetch fails, it withholds the push. 300 s after the last push, mint and redeem revert with `TwapNotAvailable` and the books switch to free float. |
| **Book manager** | 30 s | Re-centres each book on its inventory-shifted centre once it drifts past the reposition gate, and posts the bin shape with `set_pool_shape`. See [Book management](/keeper/book-management). |
| **Inventory** | With the book manager | Refills a side whose asks fall below half its bids by minting pairs (`vault_mint_pairs`), and trims asks above 1.5× the bid target. Standing bid cUSDC stays at $5,000 per side: the excess is swept to the CLP vault, and bids that sellers spent are refilled from it (`clp_fund_pool_usdc`). |
| **Parameter posting** | With the book manager | Posts the mint and redeem fee, `clamp(fee_mult × half-spread, 10, 100)` bps (`update_market`), the committed-order spread, `clamp(half-spread, 10, 500)` bps (`set_commit_spread`), the overcollateralization target (`set_buffer_target`) and the γ index (`set_s_gamma_index`). Unposted γ is saved to R2 or `DATA_DIR` and survives a restart, and a market with no γ write for a day is posted once at least 1e-6 is pending. |
| **Commit settler** | 20 s idle, 5 s while orders are pending | Finds pending committed orders, provisions inventory (sells first), and settles up to 10 per tick, largest first. Cash the orders need beyond the CLP vault comes from the treasury (`allocate_to_market`). `settle_swap` is permissionless; the keeper is the cranker that is always running. |
| **Liquidity reallocator** | 30 s | Keeps each market's CLP vault between its working need and twice it: what its bid refills, inventory restock and instant swaps can spend in one tick. Tops up from the treasury below the need and returns everything above 1.5× once a vault passes twice the need (`allocate_to_market`, `return_to_global`). |
| **Cross-market rebalance** | With the book manager | Moves collateral from markets above their target ratio to markets below it (`rebalance_collateral`). It plans at the stored price the program checks and leaves the source one confidence band of margin. A destination within one band of its target is left alone, and past it is filled to target plus the band. Markets below 100% are filled first, and each source's excess is split in proportion to need. The program clamps each move to what the source holds above its own target, so a plan made at an old price moves less than requested and never drains the source. |
| **Health** | 300 s | Watches the SOL balances of both signers. Below a critical line it tops the wallet up (a devnet airdrop, a mainnet USDC-to-SOL swap of at most $250, or a transfer from the other signer) and posts a Discord alert if the wallet is still below. |

The **yield allocator** runs every 300 s. It plans how much of each market's collateral to lend, and where, from live mainnet state of four USDC venues. It lends only on mainnet with `COLLATERAL_YIELD_ENABLED=true`, which is off by default, and elsewhere it only plans. On devnet, an emulator credits each market the interest that plan would earn. See [Keeper → Yield allocator](/concepts/keeper#yield-allocator-300s).

Every transaction that moves funds is first written to a journal in R2 or on a persistent disk. If a send ends with an unknown outcome, the keeper pauses those sends until it has reconciled the transaction and restarted. Price pushes skip the journal because repeating one is harmless.

## What it does not do

- **Set prices by hand.** Each half-spread is the largest of four terms: a realized q90 ratchet, a slow auto-floor based on 60-day volatility, oracle confidence plus a fixed cost, and a static per-market floor. No trained model, no discretionary input. See [the keeper concept page](/concepts/keeper).
- **Sit in the user's call path.** `mint_paired`, `redeem_paired`, `pool_swap`, `commit_swap` and `cancel_swap` need neither keeper key. Mint and redeem do need a price push from the last 300 s. If the keeper stops, the books keep their last posted bins, because `pool_swap` does not check price age, and the owner of an unsettled committed order can call `cancel_swap` once its 180 s TTL has passed.

## Signing roles

The keeper wallet is each market's `keeper_authority`. It pushes prices, posts buffer targets and the γ index, rebalances collateral, and can call the keeper-only single-leg instructions (`keeper_mint_single`, `keeper_redeem_single`, `keeper_burn_single`). It could also move collateral to a keeper-owned account for lending (`deploy_collateral_to_yield`) on a market with `yield_enabled` set. Every live market has it set as of 11 Oct 2026, but the keeper lends only on mainnet.

The CLP authority signs book shapes, sweeps, inventory moves and `set_commit_spread`. Fee posting goes through `update_market`, which requires the market's `authority`.
